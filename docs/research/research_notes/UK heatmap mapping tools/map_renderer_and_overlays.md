# Browser map renderers and overlay techniques for a UK £/m² heatmap with a pin-drop target property (state as of 10 October 2026)

Scope note: facts below were checked against primary sources (npm registry, GitHub releases, official docs, live page source) on 2026-10-10. Anything older than 2025 is flagged "(older)". Version/date facts pulled from the npm registry are marked [npm].

## Key question 1: Which renderer should a two-developer team pick, and why?

### Takeaway
Pick MapLibre GL JS (v6.13.0, BSD-3-Clause) as the sole renderer, with its native `fill` layer (data-driven `fill-color`) for the hex/grid cells, its native `circle` + `heatmap` layers for the street-level points, `global-state` expressions for property-type/time-window filters, and a `Marker({draggable:true})` for the target pin; use one of the two maintained Svelte 5 wrappers. Leaflet is stuck between 1.9.4 (May 2023) and an unfinished 2.0 alpha and has no native vector tiles or GPU heatmap; OpenLayers 10.11.0 is capable but heavier conceptually and its WebGL path is the less-travelled one; Google Maps JS has removed its HeatmapLayer (May 2026) and is metered. deck.gl is only worth adding if you need GPU aggregation of raw points or extruded 3D hexes (see KQ3).

Rendering model and capability fit
- Leaflet renders with SVG/Canvas, not WebGL; vector tiles only via plugins; positioned by 2026 comparisons as the choice "for simple raster maps, markers, and choropleths"; MapLibre "for modern vector-tile maps with custom styling, 3D, or a self-hosted tile stack"; OpenLayers "for projection handling, WMS/WFS-style GIS workflows" — [geoapify](https://www.geoapify.com/map-libraries-comparison-leaflet-vs-maplibre-gl-vs-openlayers-trends-and-statistics/); [jawg blog](https://blog.jawg.io/maplibre-gl-vs-leaflet-choosing-the-right-tool-for-your-interactive-map/)

- A 2025 ICA benchmark (four libraries, large GeoJSON): below ~10,000 features Leaflet and OpenLayers "excelled"; at 50,000+ features Mapbox GL JS was quickest, then OpenLayers, MapLibre, Leaflet; for Mapbox/MapLibre the per-feature cost flattens beyond ~1,000 features, and their slow initialisation makes them less suited to tiny datasets — [ICA Abstracts 2025](https://ica-abs.copernicus.org/articles/10/14/2025/ica-abs-10-14-2025.pdf). - A US government open-data project with 200,000+ block-group polygons chose MapLibre after OpenLayers "proved insufficient" — [j40-cejst decision record](https://git.lsit.ucsb.edu/publicdata/j40-cejst-2/raw/branch/main/docs/decisions/0007-maplibre.md)
- MapLibre 6.0.0 removed WebGL1 support (WebGL2 required), went ESM-only (UMD/CSP bundles dropped), style-spec 25 now throws on legacy (non-expression) styling, `Map` composes a `Camera` rather than extending it, TypeScript target ES2022 — [MapLibre CHANGELOG](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/maplibre/maplibre-gl-js/main/CHANGELOG.md)

### Inferences
- For this use case the deciding features are (a) GPU rendering of tens of thousands of data-styled polygons, (b) native vector-tile support for the national-scale layers, (c) zoom-driven cross-fades without re-adding layers, (d) first-class Svelte 5 wrappers. MapLibre has all four; Leaflet lacks (a)–(c) natively and its 2.0 transition means plugin churn; OpenLayers has (a)–(c) but no Svelte wrapper of comparable maturity and a heavier API; Google has lost its heatmap, is metered, and would need deck.gl anyway.
- The benchmark literature is contradictory on absolute MapLibre speed, but it consistently shows MapLibre cost is flat with feature count once past ~1,000 features, which is exactly the regime of "tens of thousands of cells in view".
- Dropping WebGL1 in v6 is a non-issue for UK consumer browsers in 2026 (WebGL2 has shipped in Safari since 2021), but it removes any fallback; if a WebGL-less environment matters, Leaflet/OpenLayers Canvas are the only options.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head 2026 benchmark covers exactly "~30k data-styled hexagons + hover" on mobile; the only quantitative sources are the 2025 ICA/IJGI studies above, which test raw GeoJSON loading rather than vector-tile rendering.
- Bundlephobia returned no result for `leaflet`, `ol`, `@deck.gl/aggregation-layers`, `@deck.gl/geo-layers`, `@deck.gl/maplibre` or `h3-js` at query time, so their gzip sizes above come from third-party comparisons, not a measured build.

## Key question 2: MapLibre GL JS — version, heatmap capabilities and limits, fill layer data-driven colour, vector tile vs GeoJSON thresholds, globe/recent features, Svelte bindings

### Takeaway
MapLibre 6.13.0's `heatmap` layer is a kernel-density (count) renderer, not a value interpolator, so it suits the street-level "where are the transactions" view but not a £/m² surface; the £/m² surface should be `fill` polygons (pre-rendered hexes/grid squares as vector tiles or GeoJSON) coloured with `interpolate`/`step` on `["get","median_ppm2"]`, filtered with `global-state`, and cross-faded with zoom-interpolated opacity. Both `svelte-maplibre` (2.0.1) and `svelte-maplibre-gl` (2.2.1) are Svelte-5-only, actively released in 2026, and support MapLibre 6.

Layer types and the heatmap layer
- Layer types: `background`, `fill`, `line`, `symbol`, `raster`, `circle`, `fill-extrusion`, `heatmap`, `hillshade`, `color-relief` — [style spec: layers](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-style-spec/layers/)
- `heatmap` paint properties: `heatmap-radius` (px, default 30, data-driven and feature-state capable, transitionable), `heatmap-weight` (default 1, data-driven, "a value of 10 would be equivalent to having 10 points of weight 1 in the same spot"), `heatmap-intensity` (default 1, zoom-only, not data-driven), `heatmap-color` (must use `["heatmap-density"]` as input, not data-driven), `heatmap-opacity` (0–1, not data-driven) — [style spec: layers](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-style-spec/layers/)
- `["heatmap-density"]` "Gets the kernel density estimation of a pixel in a heatmap layer, which is a relative measure of how many data points are crowded around a particular pixel" and "Can only be used in the heatmap-color property" — [style spec: expressions](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-style-spec/expressions/)
- Official heatmap example: GeoJSON source; heatmap layer `maxzoom: 9` with `heatmap-weight: ['interpolate',['linear'],['get','mag'],0,0,6,1]`, `heatmap-intensity: ['interpolate',['linear'],['zoom'],0,1,9,3]`, `heatmap-radius: ['interpolate',['linear'],['zoom'],0,2,9,20]`, `heatmap-opacity: ['interpolate',['linear'],['zoom'],7,1,9,0]`; a `circle` layer with `minzoom: 7` and `circle-opacity: ['interpolate',['linear'],['zoom'],7,0,8,1]` takes over, so the two cross-fade between z7 and z9; the page notes "Heatmap layers also work with a vector tile source" — [MapLibre heatmap example](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-gl-js/docs/examples/create-a-heatmap-layer/)
- A third-party custom layer, `@geoql/maplibre-gl-interpolate-heatmap`, renders value-interpolated (weighted-average) surfaces with WebGL2 shaders and states it works with MapLibre v5+/v6+ — [JSR package](https://jsr.io/@geoql/maplibre-gl-interpolate-heatmap)

Data-driven styling (fill, circle) and expressions
- `fill-color`, `fill-opacity`, `fill-outline-color` support data-driven expressions and `feature-state`; `fill-pattern` and `fill-sort-key` are data-driven only. Circle: `circle-radius`, `circle-color`, `circle-blur`, `circle-opacity`, `circle-stroke-width/color/opacity` are data-driven and feature-state capable — [style spec: layers](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-style-spec/layers/)
- Expression operators include `step`, `interpolate`, `interpolate-hcl`, `interpolate-lab` (perceptual colour interpolation, GL JS ≥0.49), `match`, `case`, `coalesce`, `in`, `index-of`, `slice`, `within`, `distance` (GL JS ≥4.2), `feature-state` (≥0.46), `global-state` (≥5.6.0), `elevation` (≥5.6.0), `split`/`join` (≥5.20.0), `semiliteral` (≥6.6.0) — [style spec: expressions](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-style-spec/expressions/)
- `global-state` reads a value set through the map API, falls back to defaults declared in the style root `state` property, returns `null` if neither is set; it is GL JS-only (not MapLibre Native) — [style spec: expressions](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-style-spec/expressions/)

Sources: GeoJSON vs vector tiles
- GeoJSON source options: `data` (URL or inline), `maxzoom` (default 18, "Maximum zoom level for creating vector tiles" — i.e. GeoJSON is tiled client-side), `buffer` (default 128, 0–512), `tolerance` (Douglas–Peucker, default 0.375), `filter`, `cluster` (default false) with `clusterRadius` (50), `clusterMaxZoom`, `clusterMinPoints` (2), `clusterProperties` (`{name:[operator, map_expression]}`, may use `["accumulated"]`), `generateId`, `promoteId` (property to use as feature id for feature-state) — [style spec: sources](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-style-spec/sources/)
- Vector source options: `url` (TileJSON), `tiles` (XYZ templates), `bounds` (tiles outside are never requested — useful to clamp to the UK), `scheme` (`xyz`/`tms`), `minzoom`/`maxzoom` (default 0/22; tiles at maxzoom are overzoomed for higher zooms), `promoteId`; tiles must be MVT or MapLibre Tile (MLT) format; layers on vector sources must set `source-layer`; "Features render only within their originating tile, so large width, radius, size, or offset values can cause artifacts" — [style spec: sources](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-style-spec/sources/)
- `@maplibre/mlt` is now a runtime dependency of `maplibre-gl@6.13.0` (MLT tile format support) and `6.0.0-12` covered MLT overzoomed tiles — [npm registry](https://registry.npmjs.org/maplibre-gl); [CHANGELOG](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/maplibre/maplibre-gl-js/main/CHANGELOG.md)
- Unofficial guidance (an agent-skill guide, not MapLibre docs) suggests a "lag zone" of roughly 5–20 MB / up to ~50,000 GeoJSON features where 1–3 s parse delays appear and mobile may struggle, and recommends vector tiles beyond that or whenever zoom-dependent generalisation is needed — [claudeskills maplibre-tile-sources (unofficial)](https://claudeskills.info/zh/skills/maplibre/maplibre-agent-skills/maplibre-tile-sources/)
- An OSM rails-dev thread argues MapLibre renders GeoJSON "much more performantly than Leaflet, since it doesn't involve the DOM" — [OSM rails-dev, Dec 2025](https://lists.openstreetmap.org/pipermail/rails-dev/2025-December/046212.html)

British National Grid
- EPSG:27700 is Transverse Mercator (lat origin 49°, central meridian −2°, false easting/northing 400000/−100000 m) on OSGB36, covering onshore GB plus the Isle of Man; OSGB36→WGS84 needs a grid shift (OSTN15 official; approximate transforms give up to ~5 m misalignment) — [epsg.io 27700](https://epsg.io/27700)
- The OS Maps API serves tiles in EPSG:27700 with a GB-only tile matrix set, separate from its EPSG:3857 set — [OS Maps API layers and styles](https://docs.os.uk/os-apis/accessing-os-apis/os-maps-api/layers-and-styles)
- OpenLayers 10.8.0 added generic projection lookup via spatialreference.org and 10.7.0 added VectorTile reprojection — [OpenLayers releases](https://github.com/openlayers/openlayers/releases)

### Inferences
- Layer plan that maps directly onto spec features: (1) national hex/grid cells as a `vector` source (bounds clamped to the UK) with a `fill` layer, `fill-color: ["interpolate",["linear"],["get","median_ppm2"], ...stops]` or a `["step", ...]` for classed legends, `fill-opacity: ["interpolate",["linear"],["zoom"], z_a,1, z_b,0]` for cross-fades; (2) the same for finer resolutions with inverse opacity ramps; (3) street-level `circle` layer with `circle-color` by £/m² and `minzoom`, plus optionally a `heatmap` layer for density only; (4) `global-state` for property-type and time-window filters so a single `setGlobalState` call restyles every layer without re-adding sources (and since 6.11.0 the change transitions smoothly); (5) `feature-state` with `promoteId` for hover highlight; (6) `maplibregl.Marker({draggable:true})` with `dragend` for the pin, or a `Popup` on `click`/`mousemove`.
- Because `heatmap-color` only sees density, encoding £/m² into `heatmap-weight` would conflate "expensive" with "many sales"; a true value surface needs either pre-aggregated polygons (recommended) or the third-party interpolate-heatmap custom layer.
- The practical GeoJSON/vector-tile boundary for this project: a single local-area GeoJSON of a few thousand to ~20k cells is fine (MapLibre tiles it in the worker; use `updateData` for diffs), but a national 100–250 m grid (millions of cells) must be pre-tiled as MVT/PMTiles with per-zoom resolution switching; `maxzoom` overzooming means the finest tileset need only go to the zoom where cells are ~1 px.
- For a UK-only consumer map, Web Mercator is fine: OS and all vector basemap providers serve 3857; BNG only matters if you ingest EPSG:27700 datasets (reproject once server-side with OSTN15) or want OS 27700 tiles (not supported by MapLibre, supported by OpenLayers).
- `svelte-maplibre-gl` is the cleaner choice for a greenfield Svelte 5 app (runes-first, no runtime deps, explicit MapLibre 6 peer range, deck.gl and PMTiles examples); `svelte-maplibre` has more pre-built components (JoinedData, MarkerLayer, DeckGlLayer) and a longer track record. Either is viable; both are single-maintainer/small-org projects.

### Gaps
- MapLibre does not publish an official feature-count threshold for GeoJSON sources; the 50k-feature "lag zone" figure comes from an unofficial guide.
- The 5.0.0 changelog entry (globe introduction) was beyond the fetched portion of the CHANGELOG; globe availability is confirmed indirectly via release notes and deck.gl docs.
- I did not find documentation of whether `svelte-maplibre-gl` exposes a dedicated `HeatmapLayer` component (its docs page lists examples, not the component API); `svelte-maplibre` does.

## Key question 3: deck.gl on top of MapLibre — when is it worth it, bundle cost, complexity

### Takeaway
deck.gl 9.4.0 integrates with MapLibre 4.5.1–6 through the new `@deck.gl/maplibre` module (`MapLibreOverlay`, overlaid or interleaved via `beforeId`). It is worth adding only if you need GPU-side aggregation of raw points on the fly (`HexagonLayer` with `gpuAggregation`, `ScreenGridLayer`), a GPU density `HeatmapLayer` with `MEAN` aggregation, extruded 3D hexes, or H3 cells rendered straight from indexes (`H3HexagonLayer`); it costs roughly 0.5 MB gzipped for the umbrella package and a second rendering pipeline to reason about. For pre-aggregated £/m² cells, MapLibre's own `fill` layer is sufficient.

### Cited Findings
- `@deck.gl/maplibre` is new in 9.4, "forked from `@deck.gl/mapbox`", supports MapLibre GL JS v4, v5 and v6; install `npm install @deck.gl/maplibre maplibre-gl`; `MapLibreOverlay` implements MapLibre's `IControl` and is added with `map.addControl(new MapLibreOverlay({interleaved: true, layers: [...]}))`; MapLibre owns camera, canvas size and interaction so `width`, `height`, `viewState`, `controller` etc. cannot be passed — [deck.gl MapLibre overview](https://deck.gl/docs/api-reference/maplibre/overview)
- Overlaid mode (default) renders deck.gl to a separate canvas above the map; interleaved mode shares MapLibre's WebGL2 context and lets `beforeId` insert a deck layer under a named style layer (e.g. `beforeId: 'waterway-label'`); interleaved requires WebGL2, only one interleaved overlay per map, mode is fixed at construction, and MapLibre's default `antialias: false` causes aliased edges on Path/Line/Arc/PointCloud layers unless `antialiasing: true` is set per layer or `antialias: true` on the map — [deck.gl MapLibre overview](https://deck.gl/docs/api-reference/maplibre/overview)
- Globe: deck.gl uses its experimental `GlobeView`; deck layers are not draped over MapLibre terrain; `TextLayer` and non-billboard `IconLayer` do not render with default back-face culling — [deck.gl MapLibre overview](https://deck.gl/docs/api-reference/maplibre/overview)
- 9.4 (2026-09-05): all official layers support WebGPU (experimental); apps targeting WebGL2 only can set the `visgl:webgl-only` export condition to drop WebGPU branches and WGSL shaders from the bundle. 9.3 (2026-04-13): widget overhaul, `maxBounds` on controllers. 9.2 (2025-10-07): new A5Layer; CARTO ClusterTileLayer/HeatmapTileLayer gained H3 support. 9.1 (2025-01-21): GPU aggregation returned (`HexagonLayer` `gpuAggregation: true`), generic `Aggregator` interface. 9.0 (2024-03-21, older): TypeScript types on by default, ESM — [deck.gl what's new](https://deck.gl/docs/whats-new)
- `HeatmapLayer` (`@deck.gl/aggregation-layers`): GPU Gaussian KDE; props `radiusPixels` (30), `intensity` (1), `threshold` (0.05), `colorRange` (default 6-class YlOrRd), `colorDomain` (if unset, derived from the current viewport so colours shift as you pan), `weightsTextureSize` (2048), `aggregation` `'SUM'` | `'MEAN'`, `debounceTimeout` (500 ms); on iOS Safari WebGL cannot render to float textures so an 8-bit fallback applies where weights must be integers and accumulated weight per pixel cannot exceed 255 — [deck.gl HeatmapLayer](https://deck.gl/docs/api-reference/aggregation-layers/heatmap-layer)
- `HexagonLayer`: bins points into pointy-topped hexagons with `radius` in metres (default 1000); `gpuAggregation` default true, falls back to CPU when a custom `hexagonAggregator`, `getColorValue` or `getElevationValue` is defined; `colorAggregation` `SUM|MEAN|MIN|MAX|COUNT` with `getColorWeight`; `colorScaleType` `linear|quantize|quantile|ordinal` (quantile/ordinal force a GPU→CPU readback); `colorDomain` auto by default; default binning via d3-hexbin; `extruded` for 3D columns — [deck.gl HexagonLayer](https://deck.gl/docs/api-reference/aggregation-layers/hexagon-layer)
- `H3HexagonLayer` (`@deck.gl/geo-layers`): `getHexagon` accessor returns an H3 index per object; requires `h3-js`; all cells in one layer must share a resolution; `highPrecision: 'auto'` uses instanced drawing (every hex assumed to share the viewport-centre hex's shape) and switches to `SolidPolygonLayer` high-precision mode when data has pentagons, coarse resolutions 0–5, or mixed resolutions; `coverage` radius multiplier is transition-enabled — [deck.gl H3HexagonLayer](https://deck.gl/docs/api-reference/geo-layers/h3-hexagon-layer)

- Bundle: `deck.gl@9.4.0` 552,619 B gzipped (umbrella); `@deck.gl/layers` alone 58,020 B gzipped — [bundlephobia](https://bundlephobia.com/package/deck.gl@9.4.0)
- `h3-js` 4.5.0 (published 2026-07-01), Apache-2.0, transpiled from C via emscripten, usable in browser or Node; `cellToBoundary(h3Index, formatAsGeoJson?)` returns `[lng,lat]` closed loops when the flag is true; `cellsToMultiPolygon(h3Indexes, formatAsGeoJson?)`; `polygonToCells(coordinates, res, isGeoJson?)`; `latLngToCell(lat, lng, res)`; `gridDisk`, `cellToParent`, `cellToChildren` — [h3-js README 4.5.0](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/h3-js@4.5.0/README.md); [npm h3-js](https://registry.npmjs.org/h3-js)
- Svelte wrappers: `svelte-maplibre` ships a `DeckGlLayer` component with optional `@deck.gl/* ^9` peer deps; `svelte-maplibre-gl` has a "deck.gl Overlay" example — [svelte-maplibre docs](https://svelte-maplibre.vercel.app/); [svelte-maplibre-gl docs](https://svelte-maplibre-gl.mierune.dev/)

### Inferences
- Three ways to put H3 hexes on MapLibre, in increasing complexity: (1) pre-render polygons server-side (H3 index → boundary via h3-js or the H3 Python/DuckDB bindings) into MVT/PMTiles or GeoJSON and draw with a `fill` layer — zero extra client deps, works with `global-state`/`feature-state`; (2) client-side `h3-js` `cellToBoundary(cell, true)` → GeoJSON `Polygon` features → `geojson` source + `fill` layer — fine for a few thousand cells per view, adds ~h3-js wasm to the bundle; (3) deck.gl `H3HexagonLayer` fed with `{h3, median_ppm2}` rows — fastest for very large cell counts and the only route to extruded 3D, but adds deck.gl, a second render pipeline, and its own picking/tooltip API instead of MapLibre popups.
- deck.gl's aggregation layers compute aggregates per viewport on the client from raw points; for a £/m² product whose aggregates are curated server-side (medians per cell per period/type), that is the wrong place to aggregate. `HeatmapLayer` with `aggregation: 'MEAN'` is the only deck layer that would render a mean-value surface directly from points, and its iOS 8-bit fallback (integer weights ≤255 per pixel) is awkward for £/m² weights.
- Note `@deck.gl/mapbox` is still what `svelte-maplibre` 2.0.1 lists as a peer dependency; with deck.gl 9.4 the MapLibre-specific `@deck.gl/maplibre` module is the documented path, so expect the wrapper to lag slightly.

### Gaps
- No measured gzip size was available for `@deck.gl/maplibre`, `@deck.gl/aggregation-layers` or `@deck.gl/geo-layers` individually (bundlephobia build failed); only the umbrella and `@deck.gl/layers` sizes are confirmed.
- I found no published mobile frame-rate benchmark for `H3HexagonLayer` at the 10k–50k cell scale.

## Key question 4: How production UK property maps render their maps and overlays (verified from page source where possible)

### Takeaway
Of the sites checked on 2026-10-10: Housemetric runs Mapbox GL JS v3.7.0 with a Mapbox dark style, a custom `vector` tile source and `geojson`/`circle`/`fill`/`line` layers; Rightmove's map search is Google Maps JS (dynamic `importLibrary`, Polygon/Rectangle/Polyline for draw-a-search); Housometer is a Next.js site whose "3,620 hexagons" price map is served as an image from its own API rather than a client map library; Zoopla blocked automated fetches so its stack is unverified; PropertyData's public pages load no map library at all (its tools are behind login).

## Key question 5: Accessibility and colour-blind-safe sequential scales for £/m²

### Takeaway
Use a perceptually uniform, CVD-safe sequential ramp (viridis/cividis/inferno family from matplotlib, Crameri's batlow, or ColorBrewer multi-hue YlGnBu) applied via `step` (classed) or `interpolate-lab`/`interpolate-hcl` in MapLibre, and follow GOV.UK/Scottish design-system map guidance: ≥3:1 contrast between adjacent fills and against the basemap, ≥4.5:1 for legend text, never rely on colour alone (add value labels/popups and a legend), and provide a non-visual alternative (table or text) for screen-reader users.

### Cited Findings
- viridis scales "are perceptually uniform in both colour and black-and-white" and "designed to be perceived by viewers with common forms of colour blindness"; options A–H map to magma, inferno, plasma, viridis, cividis, rocket, mako, turbo — [ggplot2 scale_colour_viridis docs](https://www.rdocumentation.org/packages/ggplot2/versions/4.0.1/topics/scale_colour_viridis_d)
- cividis is a blue→yellow sequential palette "safe for red-green deficient viewers" with monotonically increasing lightness; caveat that viridis-family palettes "have rather high chroma throughout which does not work as well for sequential palettes on a white/light background" and work better on dark backgrounds — [colorspace article (Zeileis)](https://zeileis.codeberg.page/colorspace/articles/approximations.html)
- Crameri scientific colour maps (batlow etc.) are "perceptually uniform, perceptually ordered, color vision deficiency friendly, readable as black and white print"; batlow is the general-purpose sequential one — [khroma: Crameri schemes](https://tesselle.r-universe.dev/khroma/doc/crameri.html); the 2020 Nature Communications paper (Crameri, Shephard, Heron) is the primary reference, and CEED cites ~8% of men and ~0.5% of women with a CVD — [CEED blog](https://www.mn.uio.no/ceed/english/about/blog/2020/using-better-colours-in-science.html)
- `d3-scale-chromatic` 3.1.0 (ISC) provides sequential, diverging and categorical schemes "derived from Cynthia A. Brewer's ColorBrewer", interpolated with uniform B-splines; usable as a JS source of hex stops for MapLibre expressions — [GitHub d3-scale-chromatic](https://github.com/d3/d3-scale-chromatic); [npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/d3-scale-chromatic)
- GOV.UK brand design-system map colour guidance: limit colours, ensure a 3:1 contrast ratio, combine colour with shapes/patterns/labels, include a legend, check for visually impaired and colour-blind users — [GOV.UK design system: colour in maps](https://brand.design-system.service.gov.uk/colour/maps)
- Scottish Government design system: aim for 3:1 contrast for adjacent colours, avoid using colour alone to convey meaning, choose sequential/diverging/qualitative palettes to suit the data, diverging palettes need a neutral midpoint and at least 5 colours, greyscale alone is "unlikely to have sufficient colour contrast" — [designsystem.gov.scot: using colour in maps](https://designsystem.gov.scot/guidance/maps/using-colour-in-maps)
- WCAG 2.1 non-text contrast (1.4.11) requires ≥3:1 for graphical objects and UI components; 4.5:1 applies to text; Section 508 references WCAG 2.0, which lacks the non-text criterion, so a map can pass an audit with indistinguishable fills — [Esri ArcGIS blog on accessible maps](https://www.esri.com/arcgis-blog/products/arcgis-living-atlas/mapping/towards-an-accessible-basemap); [mapcreator guide](https://mapcreator.io/see-the-world-differently-a-guide-to-colorblind-friendly-maps/)
- A 2026 study notes a screen reader typically announces a visual choropleth as just "graphic", so an alternative text/table representation is needed — [arXiv 2605.07849](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.07849)
- MapLibre provides `interpolate-hcl` and `interpolate-lab` (GL JS ≥0.49) so colour stops are blended in perceptual spaces rather than RGB, and `step` for classed ramps — [style spec: expressions](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-style-spec/expressions/)
- deck.gl aggregation layers default to a 6-class ColorBrewer YlOrRd `colorRange` and support `colorScaleType: 'quantile'` for skewed data — [deck.gl HexagonLayer](https://deck.gl/docs/api-reference/aggregation-layers/hexagon-layer)

### Inferences
- £/m² is right-skewed (London vs. the rest), so a classed `step` ramp on quantile or log breaks computed server-side will read better than linear `interpolate`; keep the same breaks across zoom levels so the legend stays valid when layers cross-fade.
- On a light basemap, prefer cividis, batlow, or ColorBrewer YlGnBu/BuPu over viridis (chroma issue above); on a dark basemap (as Housemetric uses) viridis/inferno work well. Whichever is chosen, verify adjacent-class contrast ≥3:1 and run a deuteranopia/protanopia simulation.
- Accessibility plan that costs little: a legend component in Svelte with text labels meeting 4.5:1, popups that state the numeric £/m² and sample count (so colour is never the only channel), keyboard-focusable pin and list view of the nearest cells, and `aria-label` on the map container describing what it shows.

### Gaps
- I found no independent CVD-simulation test of batlow outside its authors' materials.
- No source gave a tested colour ramp specifically for property-price maps; the guidance above is general cartographic/accessibility guidance applied to this case.
