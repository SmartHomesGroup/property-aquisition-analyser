# Build the £/m² heatmap on MapLibre, Martin and H3

*Research report, 10 October 2026. Synthesised from five research notes in `docs/research/research_notes/UK heatmap mapping tools/` (renderer, basemaps, PostGIS serving, open datasets, geocoding and Rightmove). Prices and versions are as observed on 10 October 2026 unless stated; anything older is dated.*

The right stack for a two-developer startup on one Linux VM is **MapLibre GL JS 6.13.0 rendered through the `svelte-maplibre-gl` 2.2.1 Svelte 5 wrapper, an Ordnance Survey NGD Tiles basemap on the free OpenData plan (zoom 6–15, Light or Black & White style) with a self-hosted Protomaps PMTiles extract of Great Britain (2.9 GB at z0–15) as the licence-clean fallback, PostgreSQL 17 + PostGIS 3.5 + h3-pg 4.5.0 holding precomputed per-cell £/m² quartiles, and Martin 1.16.1 serving those cells as vector tiles from a PL/pgSQL function behind nginx**. The data foundation is HM Land Registry Price Paid Data (5.5 GB, OGL v3, monthly) joined to MHCLG EPC floor areas (OGL except address fields, GOV.UK One Login required) and time-adjusted with the UK House Price Index (January 2015 = 100, revised for 12 months); expect roughly **77% of all 29 million E&W sales, and above 90% of sales since 2011, to acquire a floor area**, with HM Land Registry's new August-2026 UPRN lookup improving only future months. The pin-drop input should be postcodes.io for a free postcode centroid and OS Places API (Premium plan, within its £1,000/month free allowance) for a house-number-accurate rooftop coordinate and UPRN; a Rightmove listing must never be fetched server-side, because Rightmove's February 2026 Terms of Use prohibit any extraction "other than by means of direct human interaction", so the user types the price, address and size themselves. The two biggest unresolved risks are legal rather than technical: the OS Data Hub API terms (caching and attribution text) could not be fetched, and the EPC address-field licence only permits energy-performance purposes, which means the published map should expose aggregated £/m² and UPRN-derived coordinates, never EPC address strings. The whole stack is open-source or free-tier at prototype volumes, and the only line items that scale with traffic are OS Premium zooms (£0.0331 per 4 tiles above z15) and address lookups (OS Places or Ideal Postcodes at roughly 3–4.5p each).

## MapLibre 6.13 is the only renderer that fits; wrap it with svelte-maplibre-gl

The renderer decision is not close. `maplibre-gl` is at **6.13.0 (published 2026-10-06, BSD-3-Clause)** and shipped eight minor releases between 9 September and 6 October 2026 ([npm registry](https://registry.npmjs.org/maplibre-gl)). Leaflet's last stable release is **1.9.4 from May 2023**, with only a 2.0.0-alpha.1 (August 2025) since, it renders with SVG/Canvas rather than WebGL, and vector tiles need plugins ([npm registry](https://registry.npmjs.org/leaflet); [Leaflet releases](https://github.com/Leaflet/Leaflet/releases)). OpenLayers 10.11.0 (2026-10-04) is capable and has a native `ol/layer/Heatmap`, but its WebGL path is the less-travelled one and it lacks a Svelte wrapper of comparable maturity ([npm registry](https://registry.npmjs.org/ol)). Google Maps JS removed its HeatmapLayer in May 2026, citing "low customer usage" and pointing users at deck.gl, and replaced its $200 monthly credit with per-SKU free thresholds on 1 March 2025 ([Google Maps deprecations](https://developers.google.com/maps/deprecations); [Google billing FAQ](https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/faq)). The one directly comparable UK product, Housemetric (Land Registry + EPC £/m² down to plot level), runs Mapbox GL JS v3.7.0 with a custom `/tiles/{z}/{x}/{y}` vector source plus `fill`, `circle` and `geojson` layers, which is the same style-spec architecture MapLibre supports without Mapbox's per-load billing ([housemetric.co.uk/map page source](https://housemetric.co.uk/map); [Mapbox pricing](https://www.mapbox.com/pricing)).

Two MapLibre 6 facts shape the design. First, MapLibre 6.0.0 (22 July 2026) **dropped WebGL1, went ESM-only, and made style-spec 25 throw on legacy non-expression styling** ([MapLibre CHANGELOG](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/maplibre/maplibre-gl-js/main/CHANGELOG.md)); this is harmless for 2026 UK consumer browsers but removes any non-WebGL fallback. Second, the built-in `heatmap` layer is a kernel-density renderer: `heatmap-color` can only read `["heatmap-density"]`, "a relative measure of how many data points are crowded around a particular pixel", and `heatmap-weight` would conflate "expensive" with "many sales" ([style spec: expressions](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-style-spec/expressions/); [style spec: layers](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-style-spec/layers/)). So the £/m² surface must be **pre-aggregated polygons drawn with a `fill` layer**, with `fill-color` driven by `["interpolate-lab", ...]` or a classed `["step", ...]` on `["get","median_ppm2"]`, `fill-opacity` interpolated on `["zoom"]` to cross-fade between resolutions, `global-state` (GL JS ≥5.6.0, and since 6.11.0 paint changes from it transition smoothly) for property-type and time-window filters, and `feature-state` with `promoteId` for hover highlight ([MapLibre releases](https://github.com/maplibre/maplibre-gl-js/releases)). The native `heatmap` layer is still useful as a street-level "where are the transactions" density layer, following the official example that cross-fades heatmap into `circle` between z7 and z9 ([MapLibre heatmap example](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-gl-js/docs/examples/create-a-heatmap-layer/)). The pin is a `maplibregl.Marker({draggable:true})` with a `dragend` handler. A vector source's `bounds` option stops MapLibre requesting tiles outside the UK, and `maxzoom` overzooming means the finest cell tileset only needs to go to the zoom where cells are about a pixel wide ([style spec: sources](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-style-spec/sources/)).

For Svelte 5 there are two maintained wrappers, both Svelte-5-only and both supporting MapLibre 6. **`svelte-maplibre-gl` 2.2.1** (MIERUNE, published 2026-08-15, MIT OR Apache-2.0, peer `maplibre-gl ^5.19.0 || ^6.0.0`, zero runtime dependencies, runes-first, examples for PMTiles protocol, deck.gl overlay and Terra Draw) is the cleaner choice for a greenfield app ([npm registry](https://registry.npmjs.org/svelte-maplibre-gl); [docs](https://svelte-maplibre-gl.mierune.dev/)). **`svelte-maplibre` 2.0.1** (dimfeld, published 2026-09-25, MIT, 522 stars) has more pre-built components (`JoinedData`, `MarkerLayer`, `DeckGlLayer`, a `HeatmapLayer` component) but bundles `maplibre-gl`, `pmtiles` and `d3-geo` as dependencies and still lists `@deck.gl/mapbox` rather than the new `@deck.gl/maplibre` as its peer ([npm registry](https://registry.npmjs.org/svelte-maplibre); [svelte-maplibre docs](https://svelte-maplibre.vercel.app/)). Both are single-maintainer or small-org projects, and both require importing the worker with `?worker&url` and calling `maplibregl.setWorkerUrl()` in a `<script module>` block under Vite. Budget for **287 KB gzipped** for MapLibre itself ([bundlephobia](https://bundlephobia.com/package/maplibre-gl@6.13.0)).

deck.gl is not needed for the core product. `deck.gl` 9.4.0 (2026-09-05, MIT) adds a new `@deck.gl/maplibre` module (`MapLibreOverlay`, overlaid or interleaved via `beforeId`, MapLibre v4–v6) and is expected to be the last v9 release ([deck.gl MapLibre overview](https://deck.gl/docs/api-reference/maplibre/overview); [deck.gl what's new](https://deck.gl/docs/whats-new)). Its value is GPU-side aggregation of raw points (`HexagonLayer` with `gpuAggregation`), extruded 3D hexes, or drawing H3 cells straight from indexes with `H3HexagonLayer`; for curated server-side medians those are the wrong place to aggregate, and the umbrella package costs **553 KB gzipped** plus a second rendering pipeline with its own picking API ([bundlephobia](https://bundlephobia.com/package/deck.gl@9.4.0); [deck.gl HexagonLayer](https://deck.gl/docs/api-reference/aggregation-layers/hexagon-layer)). Its `HeatmapLayer` is the only deck layer that renders a mean-value surface directly from points, and on iOS Safari it falls back to 8-bit textures where weights must be integers summing to at most 255 per pixel, which is awkward for £/m² ([deck.gl HeatmapLayer](https://deck.gl/docs/api-reference/aggregation-layers/heatmap-layer)). Keep it as a later option for a 3D view only.

Colour and accessibility are cheap to get right up front. £/m² is right-skewed (London versus everywhere else), so compute quantile or log class breaks server-side, keep them identical across zoom levels so the legend survives cross-fades, and apply them with `step` or `interpolate-lab`. On a light basemap prefer cividis, Crameri's batlow or ColorBrewer YlGnBu, because viridis-family ramps "have rather high chroma throughout which does not work as well for sequential palettes on a white/light background" ([colorspace article](https://zeileis.codeberg.page/colorspace/articles/approximations.html); [khroma: Crameri schemes](https://tesselle.r-universe.dev/khroma/doc/crameri.html)). GOV.UK and Scottish Government map guidance both require at least **3:1 contrast between adjacent fills**, colour never used alone, and a legend; WCAG 1.4.11 sets the same 3:1 floor for graphical objects and 4.5:1 for legend text ([GOV.UK design system: colour in maps](https://brand.design-system.service.gov.uk/colour/maps); [designsystem.gov.scot](https://designsystem.gov.scot/guidance/maps/using-colour-in-maps)). Ship the numeric £/m² and sample count in every popup, and a table view of nearby cells, because a screen reader announces a choropleth as just "graphic" ([arXiv 2605.07849](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.07849)). `d3-scale-chromatic` 3.1.0 (ISC) is a convenient JS source for the hex stops ([GitHub d3-scale-chromatic](https://github.com/d3/d3-scale-chromatic)).

## OS NGD Tiles are free to zoom 15; Protomaps on your own disk is the £0 backstop

The basemap question has a clear answer for Great Britain and a caveat for Northern Ireland. **OS NGD API – Tiles on the free OS OpenData plan serves Web Mercator zooms 6–15 with "free and unlimited usage"** (throttled to 600 transactions/minute per live project), while **zooms 16–19 are Premium data** charged at **£0.0331 per transaction of 4 tiles** after the Premium plan's £1,000/month free allowance, roughly £8.28 per 1,000 premium tiles or about 30,000 premium map views per month free ([OS NGD API – Tiles: zoom levels](https://docs.os.uk/os-apis/accessing-os-apis/os-ngd-api-tiles/zoom-levels); [OS Data Hub plans](https://osdatahub.os.uk/plans); [What is the OS Data Hub?](https://www.ordnancesurvey.co.uk/developers/os-data-hub)). The OpenData tiles already include Building Parts, roads, land use and names from the `ngd-base` collection, updated weekly, so building footprints are visible before z16; z15 is about 2.4 m/pixel, which is street level ([What data is available?](https://docs.os.uk/os-apis/accessing-os-apis/os-ngd-api-tiles/what-data-is-available)). OS added **Light and Black & White house styles in October 2025**, the latter with "no fill colours at the large scales" and both explicitly "for overlaying data"; style IDs are `light-3857` and `blackwhite-3857`, editable in Maputnik, and OS's own MapLibre template rewrites the tile URL to `.../tiles/3857/{z}/{y}/{x}?key=...` (note the y-before-x order) with `minZoom: 6, maxZoom: 19` ([OS blog: new basemap styles](https://www.ordnancesurvey.co.uk/blog/new-basemap-styles-for-os-ngd); [OS NGD Tiles styles](https://docs.os.uk/os-apis/accessing-os-apis/os-ngd-api-tiles/technical-specification/styles); [OS MapLibre template](https://docs.os.uk/os-apis/accessing-os-apis/os-ngd-api-tiles/getting-started/libraries/maplibre-gl-js)). MapLibre only renders Web Mercator, so ignore the EPSG:27700 variants. The key sits in the tile URL, OS documents no origin restriction, and OS's own demo repo shows a server-side proxy with a warning that production needs access protection ([OS-Data-Hub-API-Demos](https://github.com/OrdnanceSurvey/OS-Data-Hub-API-Demos)). The OpenData attribution line is **"Contains OS data © Crown copyright and database right 2026"** ([OS OpenData](https://www.ordnancesurvey.co.uk/opendata)). Two things to watch: the older OS Vector Tile API reaches end of life in autumn 2028, OS Open Zoomstack is scheduled for end of life in spring 2028 with a successor "still in discovery", and the OS Data Hub API terms page is JavaScript-rendered and could not be read, so the caching clause is unverified; the only text found is a 2020 Cadcorp note that Premium data may be cached only for a session ([OS Vector Tile API](https://docs.os.uk/os-apis/accessing-os-apis/os-vector-tile-api); [OS product roadmap](https://www.ordnancesurvey.co.uk/products/roadmap); [OS blog 2020](https://www.ordnancesurvey.co.uk/blog/os-data-hub-to-desktop-gis)). OS NGD covers Great Britain only; Northern Ireland needs an OSM-based source.

Self-hosting Protomaps is the fully owned alternative and the natural fallback. A GB extract from the daily Protomaps planet build (139 GB on 10 October 2026), measured with `go-pmtiles 1.31.2` over bbox −8.7,49.8 → 1.9,61.0 (which also captures Northern Ireland), is **61 MB at z0–10, 348 MB at z0–12, 1.42 GB at z0–14 and 2.86 GB at the full z0–15**, extracted in under two minutes on a home connection ([Protomaps basemap downloads](https://docs.protomaps.com/basemaps/downloads); [pmtiles CLI](https://docs.protomaps.com/pmtiles/cli)). That file can sit on the VM behind nginx (any server supporting HTTP Range requests, with CORS exposing `ETag` and allowing `Range` and `If-Match`) or on Cloudflare R2 + Workers for about **$11.45/month at 10 million requests** ([Protomaps cloud storage](https://docs.protomaps.com/pmtiles/cloud-storage); [Protomaps cost calculator](https://docs.protomaps.com/deploy/cost)). The `@protomaps/basemaps` package generates full MapLibre layer stacks with `white`, `grayscale` and `black` flavours "intended for data visualization", the design is CC0, and the tiles are ODbL so OpenStreetMap attribution is required ([Protomaps flavors](https://docs.protomaps.com/basemaps/flavors); [Protomaps layers](https://docs.protomaps.com/basemaps/layers)). Builds stop at z15 and MapLibre overzooms beyond that, so building outlines above z15 depend on UK OSM completeness, which is patchy.

## Precompute cell medians in PostGIS and serve them through Martin behind nginx

For a dataset refreshed monthly, the static-versus-dynamic tile debate collapses into one architecture: precompute, then let the tile server lazily cache. Protomaps themselves say PMTiles suits "data that changes at most daily, or never" and point to PostGIS + Martin for filterable datasets, and the FOSS4G Europe 2026 talk on the subject recommends a hybrid ([Protomaps: You might not want PMTiles](https://protomaps.com/blog/you-might-not-want-pmtiles/); [FOSS4G-Europe 2026](https://talks.osgeo.org/foss4g-europe-2026/talk/8P3BMH/)). The decisive factor is combinatorics: if the UI offers N property types × M time windows × K metrics, static means N×M×K tilesets regenerated monthly, whereas a Martin function source accepts those as `?query` parameters against one `cell_stats` table. **Martin is the actively maintained PostGIS tile server in 2026**: stable **1.16.1 (9 September 2026)** with roughly monthly minor releases, **2.0.0-beta.3 (9 October 2026)**, Rust/Actix, function sources, a built-in 512 MB LRU tile cache with TTLs, PMTiles/MBTiles serving from the same process, official `ghcr.io/maplibre/martin` images with a HEALTHCHECK, and a documented reverse-proxy pattern with nginx for caching and TLS ([crates.io martin](https://crates.io/api/v1/crates/martin); [Martin releases](https://github.com/maplibre/martin/releases); [Martin architecture](https://maplibre.org/martin/architecture/)). Two cautions: the 2.0 prebuilt binaries, Debian package and Docker images **require an x86-64-v3 CPU (AVX2/BMI2/FMA)**, so check the VM's flags or stay on 1.16.x, and Martin has no built-in auth. pg_tileserv still works and shares the same `(z, x, y, …) → bytea` function contract, but it has no GitHub releases, its last functional commit was 31 January 2025 and it needs an external cache such as Varnish; Tegola's last release was v0.21.2 on 7 January 2025 with an open geoprocessing bug (#999) for non-`mvt_postgis` providers ([pg_tileserv commits](https://github.com/CrunchyData/pg_tileserv/commits/master); [pkg.go.dev tegola](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/go-spatial/tegola); [tegola releases](https://github.com/go-spatial/tegola/releases)). As a belt-and-braces measure, have the monthly ETL also run **tippecanoe 2.79.0** (`-o default.pmtiles`, one `-Z/-z` band per resolution, then `tile-join`) so the default view can be served as a static file if the database is under load ([felt/tippecanoe README](https://github.com/felt/tippecanoe)).

Grid geometry is a product decision with a clear technical default. **h3-pg is alive and now a PostGIS-umbrella project**: v4.5.0 (8 June 2026) bundles H3 core 4.5.0, fixes PostgreSQL 17+ maintenance failures, is CI-tested on PostgreSQL 14–18, and installs with `apt install postgresql-17-h3` (Ubuntu carries 4.2.3) or `pgxn install h3`; the old zachasme repo was archived on 30 December 2025 ([postgis/h3-pg releases](https://github.com/postgis/h3-pg/releases); [postgis/h3-pg README](https://github.com/postgis/h3-pg); [ubuntuupdates postgresql-17-h3](https://ubuntuupdates.org/package/core/questing/universe/base/postgresql-17-h3)). H3's advantages are one `h3index` per sale, `h3_cell_to_parent` for every coarser level, `h3_cell_to_boundary_geometry` for the polygon, and `h3_get_resolution_from_tile_zoom(z, max_h3_resolution, min_h3_resolution)` for the zoom-to-resolution mapping ([h3-pg API docs](https://github.com/postgis/h3-pg/blob/main/docs/api.md)). The mapping from desired cell sizes to H3 resolutions is approximately **res 6 (36 km², ~6.4 km wide) ≈ 5 km, res 8 (0.74 km²) ≈ 1 km, res 9 (0.105 km²) ≈ 250 m, res 10 (0.015 km²) ≈ 100 m** ([H3 resolution table](https://h3geo.org/docs/core-library/restable/)). If exact OS-style metric squares are a product requirement, `ST_SquareGrid(1000, bounds27700)` in EPSG:27700 yields true 1 km National Grid squares with zero extra extensions and exact power-of-two nesting, whereas `ST_HexagonGrid` parents never nest and a "1000 m" grid in EPSG:3857 is 530–630 m on the ground at UK latitudes ([PostGIS ST_SquareGrid](https://postgis.net/docs/ST_SquareGrid.html); [PostGIS ST_HexagonGrid](https://postgis.net/docs/ST_HexagonGrid.html)). Either way the cells are just geometry: `ST_AsMVTGeom(ST_Transform(cell, 3857), ST_TileEnvelope(z,x,y))` renders BNG cells in MapLibre fine; native EPSG:27700 tiling remains an open MapLibre roadmap item ([MapLibre roadmap: non-Mercator](https://maplibre.org/roadmap/maplibre-gl-js/non-mercator-projection)). Nesting matters less than it looks because medians are not composable: every resolution is aggregated from the sales table, not rolled up from child cells.

The SQL pattern (author-composed from the function docs, not yet executed) is `percentile_cont(ARRAY[0.25,0.5,0.75]) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY ppm2_adj)` grouped by `(res, cell)` with a **minimum of 5 sales per cell**, which is the ONS small-area house price rule ("fewer than five sales records ... withheld"), always shipping `n` as a tile property so the client can fade thin cells ([PostgreSQL 17 aggregate functions](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/17/functions-aggregate.html); [ONS HPSSA QMI](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/housing/methodologies/housepricestatisticsforsmallareasqmi)). Note that `percentile_cont` has no partial-aggregate mode, so it will not parallelise across workers; expect minutes, not hours, for ~20M rows × 5 resolutions, but it is unmeasured.

```sql
WITH base AS (
  SELECT h3_latlng_to_cell(ST_Transform(geom, 4326), 10) AS cell10, ppm2_adj
  FROM sale_adj WHERE date_of_transfer >= current_date - interval '24 months'
), rolled AS (
  SELECT r.res, h3_cell_to_parent(b.cell10, r.res) AS cell, b.ppm2_adj
  FROM base b CROSS JOIN (VALUES (6),(7),(8),(9),(10)) AS r(res)
)
INSERT INTO cell_stats (res, cell, n, p25_ppm2, median_ppm2, p75_ppm2, geom)
SELECT res, cell, n, pct[1], pct[2], pct[3],
       ST_Transform(h3_cell_to_boundary_geometry(cell), 3857)
FROM (SELECT res, cell, count(*) AS n,
             percentile_cont(ARRAY[0.25,0.5,0.75]) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY ppm2_adj) AS pct
      FROM rolled GROUP BY res, cell HAVING count(*) >= 5) s;
```

The Martin function source then selects the resolution per tile and returns MVT; Martin passes URL query parameters as a JSON argument and reads `minzoom`/`maxzoom` from a JSON `COMMENT ON FUNCTION` ([Martin function sources](https://maplibre.org/martin/sources-pg-functions/); [PostGIS ST_AsMVT](https://postgis.net/docs/ST_AsMVT.html); [PostGIS ST_TileEnvelope](https://postgis.net/docs/ST_TileEnvelope.html)):

```sql
CREATE OR REPLACE FUNCTION public.ppm2_hex(z integer, x integer, y integer, query json DEFAULT '{}')
RETURNS bytea LANGUAGE plpgsql STABLE PARALLEL SAFE AS $$
DECLARE r integer := h3_get_resolution_from_tile_zoom(z, max_h3_resolution => 10, min_h3_resolution => 6);
        env geometry := ST_TileEnvelope(z, x, y); mvt bytea;
BEGIN
  SELECT ST_AsMVT(t, 'ppm2', 4096, 'geom') INTO mvt
  FROM (SELECT ST_AsMVTGeom(c.geom, env, 4096, 64, true) AS geom,
               c.cell::text AS h3, c.n, c.median_ppm2, c.p25_ppm2, c.p75_ppm2
        FROM cell_stats c
        WHERE c.res = r AND c.geom && ST_TileEnvelope(z, x, y, margin => 0.02)) t;
  RETURN mvt;
END $$;
COMMENT ON FUNCTION public.ppm2_hex IS '{"description":"HPI-adjusted £/m² by H3 cell","minzoom":5,"maxzoom":14}';
```

HPI time-adjustment is a two-row lookup: `price_adj = price × Index(area, ref_month) / Index(area, sale_month)` using the property-type-specific column (`DetachedIndex`, `SemiDetachedIndex`, `TerracedIndex`, `FlatIndex`) at the local-authority `AreaCode`, falling back to the all-types `Index` for PPD type "O" or blank cells. The catch is that **PPD carries a District name while HPI carries an ONS GSS code**, so the ETL needs a name-to-code crosswalk (ONSPD/NSPL `oslaua` or a hand-maintained table) that survives the 2019–2023 local-government reorganisations, and because HPI revises the previous 12 months every month, `sale_adj` must be rebuilt rather than appended ([About the UK HPI](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/about-the-uk-house-price-index/about-the-uk-house-price-index)). The comparables query is a `ST_DWithin(geom, pin, 1000)` filter in EPSG:27700 metres plus `ORDER BY geom <-> pin LIMIT 10`, which uses the GiST index only when the operator is in `ORDER BY` and the pin is a constant or parameter rather than a CTE ([PostGIS ST_DWithin](https://postgis.net/docs/ST_DWithin.html); [PostGIS KNN](https://postgis.net/docs/geometry_distance_knn.html)); restrict to the same property type, floor area ±15% and the last 24 months, and return both nominal and HPI-adjusted £/m². There is no ready-made open-source PostGIS + MapLibre £/m² hex map to lift; the nearest prior art is `kianharia30/RE-Maps` (MIT, FastAPI + PostGIS + MapLibre with eight zoom tiers and a precomputed `area_stats` table) and `huwd/houseprices` (DuckDB pipeline that already does the PPD↔EPC floor-area join via UPRN), whose code licence is not visible and must be checked before copying ([RE-Maps](https://github.com/kianharia30/RE-Maps); [huwd/houseprices](https://github.com/huwd/houseprices)). For the load itself, DuckDB reads the 5.5 GB PPD CSV and EPC files directly and psycopg 3 `COPY` is the documented fast path into Postgres; write WKB or GeoParquet rather than relying on DuckDB's undocumented PostGIS geometry handling ([DuckDB postgres extension](https://duckdb.org/docs/current/core_extensions/postgres/overview.html); [psycopg 3 COPY](https://www.psycopg.org/psycopg3/docs/basic/copy.html)).

The join from a sale to a floor area is the core of the product and its coverage ceiling is well documented. PPD has no UPRN column; HM Land Registry's new lookup applies only to transactions published from 28 August 2026 and "will not provide back-dated data" ([technical specification](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistical-data-sets/technical-specification-transaction-unique-identifier-and-uprn-look-up-table-dataset)); my ratio of the August lookup (84,148 rows) to the August monthly file (90,867 rows) suggests roughly 92–93% of new transactions receive one. EPCs have carried UPRN since November 2021, at **92.4% coverage in the end-2021 release and 97.8% by mid-2022**, with address matching switched to OS NGD in March 2025 ([Owen Boswarva, Mar 2022](https://owenboswarva.com/blog/post-hou4.htm); [Owen Boswarva, Sep 2022](https://owenboswarva.com/blog/post-hou6.htm)). For history, therefore, the route is an address match: block on postcode, compare normalised PAON/SAON/Street against EPC ADDRESS1–3, resolve one-to-many by the EPC lodged nearest the sale date with a non-null `TOTAL_FLOOR_AREA`, and carry the EPC's UPRN forward to OS Open UPRN for a rooftop coordinate. Published results bound expectations: Powell-Smith's 2017 normalised-address join matched **79%** of sales since 2007 ([houseprices.anna.ps, archived](http://web.archive.org/web/20190409053622/http://houseprices.anna.ps/)); UCL's 251-rule four-stage matcher achieved **93.15% for 2011–2019 but only 56.2% in 2008**, with flats, City of London (71.65%) and Isles of Scilly the weak spots, and after floor-area cleaning retained 79.11% of full-market sales ([UCL Open: Environment preprint](https://journals.uclpress.co.uk/ucloepreprints/repository/object/229/download/82)); huwd/houseprices matched **76.9% of ~29.3M PPD rows** in March 2026 via a three-tier UPRN match ([huwd/houseprices](https://github.com/huwd/houseprices)); and UBDC reports **96%** achievable for both EPC and PPD UPRN assignment with rules-based matching, noting "Flats were particularly problematic" ([UBDC news](https://ubdc.ac.uk/news/key-solutions-to-unlock-the-power-of-location-data)). The practical toolkit is Robin Linacre's `uk_address_matcher` (MIT, DuckDB + Splink, ~100,000 addresses in 30 s after preprocessing), not UCL's R code, which is **CC BY-NC and "not allowed to be used commercially"** ([GitHub uk_address_matcher](https://github.com/RobinL/uk_address_matcher); [bin-chi.github.io](https://bin-chi.github.io/Link-LR-PPD-and-Domestic-EPCs/)). Apply UCL's published sanity filters (floor area 9–974 m², £/m² 200–50,000, 6.51–100 m² per habitable room, ≤20 rooms), which removed 15% of their linked records, 99.6% of those for null or zero floor areas, and remember that EPC size errors are common (one commercial report claims one in four mis-state size by more than 10%) ([The Construction Index](https://www.theconstructionindex.co.uk/news/view/mismeasurement-makes-15-of-epc-ratings-wrong)).

Three EPC facts will bite a pipeline that ignores them. In **March 2026 `LMK_KEY` was removed in favour of `CERTIFICATE_NUMBER`** with no bridge between them; the old `epc.opendatacommunities.org/docs/*` URLs now 301 to the new beta service; and about 25% of unique UPRNs carry more than one certificate, so deduplicate on latest `LODGEMENT_DATETIME` per UPRN ([changes to format and methodology](https://get-energy-performance-data.communities.gov.uk/guidance/changes-to-the-format-and-methodology); [data dictionary](https://get-energy-performance-data.communities.gov.uk/guidance/data-dictionary)). The licence line deserves the most care: the EPC address fields may be used only for listed purposes such as "understanding building efficiency in sale or rental markets" and "Supporting property market transparency"; a £/m² map arguably fits but that is an inference, so the safe design uses EPC addresses internally for matching and publishes only aggregated values, UPRNs and coordinates ([licensing restrictions](https://get-energy-performance-data.communities.gov.uk/guidance/licensing-restrictions)). The same caution applies to the PPD address fields, where "displaying residential property price information services" is an explicitly permitted use.

Scotland and Northern Ireland cannot be done at sale level for free. Registers of Scotland charges roughly **£675–£1,465 + VAT per month** for sales-data reports (two RoS pages disagree, and both sit behind bot protection) while publishing free monthly aggregate statistics; Scotland's domestic EPC extract is free and quarterly (Q3 2016 to Q2 2026, 7z 189.9 MB, with UPRN fields) ([RoS sales data reports](https://www.ros.gov.uk/data-and-statistics/sales-and-bespoke-data/sales-data-reports); [data.gov.scot EPC](https://data.gov.scot/dataset/domestic_energy_performance_certificates___dataset_to_q1_2026)). Northern Ireland has no open transaction-level prices and its EPC data exists only as an EIR response hosted on Box with no stated licence ([DoF EIR response](https://www.finance-ni.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2026-06/DOF%202026-0254%20EIR%20Response._0.pdf)). UK HPI covers both at country and local-authority level, which is the realistic ceiling outside England and Wales.

## Pins come from postcodes.io and OS Places; a Rightmove page is read by the user, not the server

The cheapest compliant pin-input stack has two tiers. For a **postcode-only pin**, postcodes.io (MIT software, no key, ONSPD August 2026 and OS Open Names July 2026 loaded, Docker self-hostable) returns the postcode-unit centroid as lat/long plus OSGB eastings/northings, LSOA/MSOA/LA codes and a `terminated` record for old postcodes; it publishes no numeric rate limit, so self-host it on the VM for production ([postcodes.io docs](https://postcodes.io/docs/overview); [GitHub postcodes.io](https://github.com/ideal-postcodes/postcodes.io)). Show that pin with a visible "approximate" radius, because a postcode unit is typically a street segment of around 15 addresses. For a **house-number-accurate pin**, there is no free address-text-to-coordinate source in the UK: OS Open UPRN has coordinates but no addresses, OS Names API (free on the OpenData plan, "Free for all to use for any purpose") searches places, roads and postcodes but "won't return a specific address in detail", and UK OpenStreetMap had only about 1.6 million house numbers as of 2018 against 14 million in Germany, so Nominatim, Photon and Pelias are poor fits and the public Nominatim server bans client-side autocomplete anyway ([OS Names API](https://www.ordnancesurvey.co.uk/products/os-names-api); [Talk-GB Dec 2020](https://lists.openstreetmap.org/pipermail/talk-gb/2020-December/025793.html); [OSMF Nominatim policy](https://operations.osmfoundation.org/policies/nominatim/)). The answer is **OS Places API on the (free-to-join) Premium plan**, whose `Find` endpoint returns a `MATCH` score (Exact 1.0, Good 0.8–0.9, Partial 0.7) with `UPRN, ADDRESS, X_COORDINATE, Y_COORDINATE, LNG, LAT` and an `RPC` coordinate-accuracy flag, and whose `Postcode` endpoint returns a pick-list of every address in a postcode; single-listing volumes will sit well inside the **£1,000/month free premium allowance**, though the per-transaction price for Places could not be retrieved from OS's JavaScript-rendered plans page ([OS Places API datasets](https://docs.os.uk/os-apis/accessing-os-apis/os-places-api/datasets); [OS Places product page](https://www.ordnancesurvey.co.uk/products/os-places-api); [What is the OS Data Hub?](https://www.ordnancesurvey.co.uk/developers/os-data-hub)). The commercial alternative is Ideal Postcodes at **£0.045 per lookup (200 credits for £9) down to £0.028 (32,100 for £900)** with free as-you-type search, 50 trial credits, and "Free UPRN & Rooftop Geocodes with every UK address lookup"; the former budget option getAddress.io **ceased operating on 4 February 2026** after the High Court found it had infringed Ideal Postcodes' database rights ([Ideal Postcodes pricing](https://ideal-postcodes.co.uk/pricing); [Ideal Postcodes: getAddress alternative](https://blog.ideal-postcodes.co.uk/getaddress-alternative); [Solicitors Journal](https://www.solicitorsjournal.com/sjarticle/high-court-rules-in-ideal-postcodes-case)). Google Geocoding is $5.00 per 1,000 with 10,000 free per month and Autocomplete $2.83 per 1,000 with session-based waivers; Mapbox Permanent Geocoding is $5.00 per 1,000 with no free tier and results may not be redistributed ([Google Maps Platform pricing](https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/pricing); [Mapbox pricing](https://www.mapbox.com/pricing)). Loqate's 4–6.9p per lookup is uncompetitive for this use.

What a Rightmove listing exposes, and what you may do with it, are different questions. Third-party technical sources agree that a page at `/properties/{id}` embeds a `PAGE_MODEL` object whose `propertyData` holds `address.displayAddress` (street-level), `address.outcode` and `address.incode` (the incode present only "where published"), `location.latitude/longitude`, price, bedrooms, property sub-type, floorplans, an EPC image URL and sometimes `floorAreaSqm`; the numeric ID itself reveals nothing ([Scrapfly guide, 5 Oct 2026](https://scrapfly.io/blog/posts/how-to-scrape-rightmove); [Apify scraper schema](https://apify.com/unfenced-group/rightmove-co-uk-scraper.md)). **Rightmove's Terms of Use (last updated February 2026)** prohibit "bots, crawlers, scrapers or other automated programs or means to access or collect data" (cl. 5.2), bar extracting information "other than by means of direct human interaction" and limit access to a browser or Rightmove's app absent written consent (cl. 5.5), forbid using the Platform "for any other research purposes related to the property market" (cl. 5.6), reserve all database rights (cl. 8), and bar links from "any other website, application, extension, plug in, or computer program" without consent (cl. 13.4) ([Rightmove Terms of Use](https://www.rightmove.co.uk/c/terms-of-use/)). A server-side fetch of a pasted listing URL clearly breaches cl. 5.2 and 5.5; a browser extension that reads the page on the user's behalf is "technical means" and sits in a grey zone that Property Log (~100,000 users, crowdsourced price changes, "in no way affiliated with Rightmove") and PaTMa Property Insights (v2.12.0, July 2026) occupy on Rightmove's tolerance rather than any licence, with Rightmove's observable response being markup churn, not litigation ([propertylog.net](https://www.propertylog.net/); [ChromeBoard: PaTMa](https://chromeboard.com/extension/patma-property-insights-gbppndfkbhfpmpidebmnhkofloklojii)). The only path the terms leave untouched is the user reading the listing in one tab and typing postcode, house number, asking price, bedrooms and floor area into the app in another. That is the input the product should be designed around, with the OS Places pick-list confirming the building and surfacing its UPRN so the app can then pull that property's own EPC floor area and prior sales from its database.

## Recommended stack

The concrete stack, with versions as of 10 October 2026, is: **frontend** Svelte 5 + Vite with `svelte-maplibre-gl` 2.2.1 over `maplibre-gl` 6.13.0, a `fill` layer per H3 resolution band with `interpolate-lab`/`step` colour from server-computed quantile breaks, zoom-interpolated `fill-opacity` cross-fades, `global-state` for filters, `feature-state` hover, a native `heatmap` → `circle` density layer at street zooms, a draggable `Marker` for the pin, a Svelte legend meeting 4.5:1 text contrast, and `d3-scale-chromatic` for cividis/batlow/YlGnBu stops. **Basemap** OS NGD API – Tiles `light-3857` or `blackwhite-3857` on the OpenData plan (z6–15) via a small key-hiding proxy, `maxZoom` capped at 15 unless the product pays for Premium; a GB Protomaps PMTiles extract (`pmtiles extract ... --bbox=-8.7,49.8,1.9,61.0 --maxzoom=15`, 2.9 GB, re-run weekly by cron) on the VM behind nginx as the Northern Ireland and outage fallback, styled with `@protomaps/basemaps` `grayscale`. **Database** PostgreSQL 17 + PostGIS 3.5 + h3-pg 4.5.0 on Ubuntu 24.04, `sale` in EPSG:27700 with GiST, `ukhpi` unpivoted by (area_code, month, property_type), a `sale_adj` materialized view rebuilt monthly, and a `cell_stats (res, cell, n, p25, median, p75, geom3857)` table for H3 res 6–10 with n ≥ 5. **Tiles** Martin 1.16.1 (or 2.0 once stable, after checking for x86-64-v3) in Docker with `cache.size_mb` raised and `tile_expiry` in days, behind nginx `proxy_cache` and TLS, serving the `ppm2_hex` function source plus the monthly tippecanoe 2.79.0 PMTiles of the default view. **API** FastAPI (as RE-Maps does) for the comparables query and cell-detail JSON, never for tiles. **ETL** monthly, after the PPD release on the 20th working day: DuckDB reads PPD, EPC bulk, OS Open UPRN, ONSPD and the HMLR UPRN lookup; `uk_address_matcher` resolves historical sales to EPC addresses and UPRNs; psycopg 3 `COPY` loads Postgres; SQL rebuilds `sale_adj` and `cell_stats`; Martin is restarted or its cache expired. **Pin input** self-hosted postcodes.io for the centroid tier and OS Places `Find`/`Postcode` (Premium plan, free allowance) for the rooftop tier, with Ideal Postcodes as the swap-in if OS Places volume or terms become a problem. **Attribution footer** must carry, verbatim, the HM Land Registry, OS, Royal Mail and ONS lines quoted in the dataset table, plus the OS tiles line and (when the Protomaps fallback is active) OpenStreetMap ODbL attribution. **Geographic scope** England and Wales at sale level; Scotland and Northern Ireland at local-authority level from UK HPI only, and Northern Ireland postcodes excluded from any commercial lookup.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the project from "which map library" to "which licences". Every technical component has a clear 2026 winner that is free and self-hostable (MapLibre 6, Martin, h3-pg under the PostGIS umbrella, Protomaps PMTiles, postcodes.io), and the performance envelope of a few hundred pre-built polygons per tile is trivial for one VM once nginx and Martin's cache absorb repeat requests. What actually constrains the product are three legal texts the team has not yet read in full: the OS Data Hub API terms, the EPC address-field notice, and Rightmove's February 2026 terms. The design that survives all three is the one recommended here: aggregate server-side, publish only cells, UPRNs and coordinates, proxy the OS key, and let the user, not the server, read Rightmove.

The second insight is that the August 2026 arrival of HM Land Registry's UPRN lookup changes the pipeline's trajectory rather than its present. Historical £/m² coverage is capped around 77–80% overall and 90%+ from 2011 by address matching, and those figures will not improve retroactively; but every month from now on, 92–93% of new transactions arrive with an authoritative UPRN, so the product's freshest data, the data that matters most for a pin-drop valuation, will be its cleanest. Building the ETL around UPRN as the primary key from day one, with address matching as the backfill rather than the main path, positions the comparables query and the EPC join to get better every month without further engineering.
